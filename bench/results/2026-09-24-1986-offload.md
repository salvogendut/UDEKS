# Initial `1986` offload crossover results

Status: **preliminary, emulator-only, not an executive-CPU decision**.

Three runs per configuration produced byte-identical 416-byte result blocks.
All 24 records set all four validation bits, so 96 timed paths completed with
the expected mailbox sequence/result and independently computed buffer
checksum. No CIA timer overflowed.

## 1 MHz 8502 crossover summary

At 1 MHz, an 8502 executive benefits from Z80 copy at the first tested size of
128 bytes, but not from the checksum or transform through 2 KiB. A Z80
executive does not benefit from 1 MHz 8502 copy through 2 KiB; its first wins
are 256 bytes for checksum and 128 bytes for transform. The complete raw timing
arrays for both speeds are in the adjacent JSON file.

## Interpretation

For these assembly mechanisms, 2 MHz 8502 execution dominates the stock Z80
strongly enough that an 8502 executive should keep all three operations local
through 2 KiB. The reverse architecture can profitably delegate to the 8502,
but only above operation-specific thresholds. At 2 KiB, Z80-to-8502 delegation
reduces copy from 24,682 to 16,939 ticks, checksum from 53,426 to 24,117, and
transform from 61,624 to 23,087.

This strengthens—but does not settle—the 8502 executive case. Earlier
compiler-generated policy workloads favored SDCC/Z80, while these deliberately
handwritten mechanisms favor the 8502. Thresholds are properties of the exact
operation, ABI, memory placement, and machine mode; they must not be generalized
to arbitrary Z80 computation. VICE and physical C128 runs remain required.
