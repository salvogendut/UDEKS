# Initial `1986` interrupt-service results

Status: **preliminary, emulator-only, not an executive-CPU decision**.

The service suite completed three identical runs per configuration. Every run
handled 48 CIA1 Timer-A interrupts, reported no unexpected source, observed ICR
`$81` at both boundaries, incremented the 32-bit tick exactly 16 times, set its
event signal, and reached the indirect dispatch target exactly 16 times.

## Paired service cost

The most stable comparison is the paired interval from the first timestamp
after the ISR register prologue through resumption after RTI:

| Path | 8502 1 MHz | 8502 2 MHz | Z80 stock | Z80 / 2 MHz 8502 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| minimal | 247 | 123 | 425 | 3.46× |
| kernel tick | 268 | 134 | 475 | 3.54× |
| jump-table dispatch | 274 | 137 | 466 | 3.40× |

Values are median one-megahertz system ticks; lower is better. Subtracting the
paired timestamps removes the variable emulator delay before handler entry.
The minimal path still contains result instrumentation shared by all variants.

## Instrumented handler interval

The interval from the post-prologue timestamp to the timestamp immediately
after acknowledgement, validation, result storage, and variant work was:

| Path | 8502 1 MHz | 8502 2 MHz | Z80 stock |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| minimal | 115 | 57 | 208 |
| kernel tick | 136 | 68 | 258 |
| jump-table dispatch | 142 | 71 | 249 |

Relative to the minimal path, the 2 MHz 8502 spent 11 additional ticks on the
kernel tick and 14 on dispatch. The stock Z80 spent 50 and 41 additional ticks.
The post-measurement bookkeeping, wait-loop patch, epilogue, RTI, and resumed
timestamp cost a constant 66 ticks on the 2 MHz 8502, 132 at 1 MHz, and 217 on
the Z80.

## Interpretation and limitations

This is the first benchmark slice favoring the 8502 in an executive-specific
operation. Its current interrupt paths are about 3.4–3.5 times cheaper than the
stock Z80 paths after the prologue. That weighs against the Z80's earlier
compiled-C throughput and size advantage.

It remains provisional:

- The 8502 contract preserves hardware-stacked P/PC and software-stacked A/X/Y.
  The Z80 contract uses IM1 with hardware-stacked PC and software-stacked
  AF/BC/DE/HL/IX/IY; alternate registers and I/R are outside this ISR ABI.
- Absolute intervals include common measurement code. Variant differences are
  cleaner than treating the absolute values as production handler costs.
- The fixed wait-jump patch is performed after the pre-exit timestamp and is
  included in the paired service and return-tail figures.
- Underflow-to-entry measurements retain `1986` scheduling artifacts. The
  paired internal intervals are stable, but VICE and real hardware are still
  required.
